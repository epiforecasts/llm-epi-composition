# Project Objective

Your primary goal is to determine the time-varying effective reproduction number, denoted as $R_t$. This metric represents the average count of secondary infections originating from an individual infected at a specific time $t$. The estimation must be derived from the provided observational data.

## Input Data Specifications

The input data covers a 150-day observation window, commencing on 2023-01-01 and concluding on 2023-05-30. This information is accessible in the file `data/cases.csv`. It contains two columns: `date`, formatted as YYYY-MM-DD, and `cases`, which lists integer counts.

## Fixed Epidemiological Parameters

Certain epidemiological parameters, established through external pathogen studies, are provided and should be considered fixed and certain; no uncertainty propagation for these values is required.

-   The generation interval follows a Gamma distribution, with a mean of 5.5 days and a standard deviation of 2 days.
-   The reporting delay, defined as the time from infection to case observation, is characterized by a log-normal distribution, possessing a mean of 5 days and a standard deviation of 2 days.

## Inherent Data Characteristics

The dataset exhibits several intrinsic characteristics:

-   A reporting delay exists between the actual infection event and its observation as a case, as specified by the distribution above.
-   The case counts are strictly integer values.
-   These counts display day-of-week variability, suggesting lower reporting rates during weekends.
-   The observed noise level exceeds what a simple homogeneous Poisson model would predict.

## Required Output and Implementation Flexibility

Your solution should generate daily point estimates for $R_t$ across the entirety of the observation period. You are free to employ any programming language, framework, or analytical methodology. The final $R_t$ estimates must be saved to `outputs/rt_estimates.csv`. This output file should include the following columns:

-   `date` (in YYYY-MM-DD format)
-   `Rt_median` (representing the point estimate)
-   `Rt_lower` (an optional, but recommended, 5th-percentile credible or confidence bound)
-   `Rt_upper` (an optional, but recommended, 95th-percentile credible or confidence bound)

## Error and Plausibility Management

Should the code encounter errors, identify and resolve them. If the resulting estimates appear implausible, it is expected that the model will be adjusted and the process re-executed.
