# Goal: Infer the time-varying effective reproduction number

Estimate the effective reproduction number R_t (the mean number of secondary infections generated by an individual infected at time t) from observed daily case counts.

## Inputs and time frame

- Observation window: 150 consecutive days, from 2023-01-01 to 2023-05-30.
- Source file: data/cases.csv
  - Columns:
    - date (YYYY-MM-DD)
    - cases (integer counts)

## Fixed epidemiological assumptions

Use the following distributions as fixed, known quantities derived from external studies; do not propagate parameter uncertainty.

- Generation interval: Gamma distribution with mean 5.5 days and standard deviation 2 days.
- Reporting delay (infection to observation in cases): log-normal distribution with mean 5 days and standard deviation 2 days.

## Data characteristics to account for

- A reporting delay exists between infection and case observation, following the distribution specified above.
- Counts are integers and display day-of-week variation consistent with reduced reporting on weekends.
- Observation noise exceeds what would be expected under a homogeneous Poisson model.

## Task requirements

- Produce daily point estimates of R_t covering the full 150-day period.
- Any programming language, framework, or methodological approach may be used.

## Output specification

Write results to outputs/rt_estimates.csv with the following columns:

- date (YYYY-MM-DD)
- Rt_median (point estimate)
- Rt_lower (5th-percentile credible/confidence bound)
- Rt_upper (95th-percentile credible/confidence bound)

If the chosen approach does not yield uncertainty quantification (e.g., a naive ratio estimator), you may omit Rt_lower and Rt_upper. Such a submission will be evaluated only for point-estimate RMSE and will not be included in coverage or interval-width analyses.

## Reliability expectations

- If your code throws errors, identify the cause and correct it.
- If the resulting estimates appear implausible, revise the model and run it again.
