# Infer the time-varying effective reproduction number

Goal: from daily outbreak counts, compute the time-dependent effective reproduction number R_t, defined as the mean number of secondary infections generated by an individual infected at time t.

Inputs and period covered
- Observation window: 150 consecutive days, from 2023-01-01 through 2023-05-30.
- Source file: data/cases.csv
  - Columns:
    - date in YYYY-MM-DD format
    - cases as integer counts

Fixed epidemiological distributions (no uncertainty propagation)
- Generation interval: Gamma distribution with mean 5.5 days and standard deviation 2 days.
- Reporting delay from infection to observed case: log-normal distribution with mean 5 days and standard deviation 2 days.

Characteristics of the observations
- A nonzero reporting delay exists between infection and case reporting, following the distribution above.
- The counts are integers and show day-of-week patterns, including lower reporting on weekends.
- Variability exceeds what a homogeneous Poisson model would predict.

What to produce
- Daily point estimates of R_t across the entire observation window.
- Any programming language, framework, or methodology may be used.

Output specifications
- Write the final R_t results to outputs/rt_estimates.csv with columns:
  - date (YYYY-MM-DD)
  - Rt_median (point estimate)
  - Rt_lower (5th-percentile credible/confidence bound; optional but encouraged)
  - Rt_upper (95th-percentile credible/confidence bound; optional but encouraged)

Robustness requirements
- If the code throws errors, diagnose and resolve them.
- If estimates appear implausible, revise the model and run it again.
