Estimate the effective reproduction number over time (Rt) from daily outbreak case counts, incorporating structured observation.

Scope and data window
- Analyze 150 consecutive days spanning 2023-01-01 through 2023-05-30.

Input data
- File: `data/cases.csv`
- Columns: `date` (YYYY-MM-DD) and `cases` (integer counts)

Fixed epidemiological assumptions (treat these as known; no uncertainty propagation)
- Generation interval: Gamma distribution with mean 5.5 days and standard deviation 2 days.
- Reporting delay from infection to case observation: log-normal distribution with mean 5 days and standard deviation 2 days.

Key characteristics of the observations
- Case reporting is delayed relative to infection according to the distribution above.
- Counts exhibit a weekly pattern due to reduced weekend reporting.
- The fraction of infections that are reported as cases (ascertainment) changes over time; this trajectory is unknown and must be inferred from the data.
- Observation noise is overdispersed compared with a homogeneous Poisson process.

Modeling requirements
- Produce daily point estimates of Rt across the full analysis period.
- Base the model on the renewal equation.
- Link the latent infection process to observed case counts via the reporting delay.
- Explicitly account for:
  - day-of-week reporting variation,
  - time-varying ascertainment, and
  - overdispersed observation noise (e.g., negative binomial).
- You may implement this in any language or framework.

Output
- Write final Rt estimates to `outputs/rt_estimates.csv` with the following columns:
  - `date` (YYYY-MM-DD)
  - `Rt_median` (point estimate)
  - `Rt_lower` (5th-percentile credible/confidence bound, optional but encouraged)
  - `Rt_upper` (95th-percentile credible/confidence bound, optional but encouraged)

Quality control
- If the code encounters errors, identify the cause and correct them.
- If the resulting estimates appear implausible, revise the model accordingly and rerun.
