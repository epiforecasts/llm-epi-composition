# Time-varying reproduction number estimation accounting for structured observation patterns

You will analyze daily infectious disease case counts from an outbreak. The objective is to estimate $R_t$, the time-varying effective reproduction number, which represents the mean number of secondary infections generated by an individual infected at time $t$.

## Input data

Daily observations span 150 days from 2023-01-01 through 2023-05-30. The input file is:

- `data/cases.csv` containing columns `date` (format: YYYY-MM-DD) and `cases` (integer).

## Fixed epidemiological parameters

The following parameters are derived from external pathogen studies and should be treated as known constants without uncertainty propagation:

- Generation interval: Gamma distribution with mean 5.5 days and standard deviation 2 days.
- Reporting delay (time from infection to case observation): log-normal distribution with mean 5 days and standard deviation 2 days.

## Data characteristics

- Case observations are delayed relative to infection times according to the reporting delay distribution specified above.
- Weekly periodicity is present in the counts due to diminished weekend reporting.
- The ascertainment rate (fraction of infections observed as cases) changes over time and is not known a priori; it must be inferred from the data.
- Observation noise exhibits overdispersion compared to a homogeneous Poisson process.

## Requirements

Generate daily point estimates of $R_t$ covering the entire observation period.

**Your model must be based on the renewal equation.** Link the unobserved infection process to the observed case data through the reporting delay. Explicitly incorporate:

- day-of-week effects in reporting,
- time-varying ascertainment, and
- overdispersed observation noise (such as negative binomial).

Any language or framework is permitted.

Write the final $R_t$ estimates to `outputs/rt_estimates.csv` with the following columns:

- `date` (format: YYYY-MM-DD)
- `Rt_median` (point estimate)
- `Rt_lower` (5th-percentile credible/confidence bound, optional but encouraged)
- `Rt_upper` (95th-percentile credible/confidence bound, optional but encouraged)

If errors occur during execution, identify and resolve them. If outputs appear implausible, modify the model and execute again.
