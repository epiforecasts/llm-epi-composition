### Objective: Time-Varying Reproduction Number Estimation

From a given set of daily case counts originating from an infectious disease outbreak, your objective is to determine the time-varying effective reproduction number, denoted as $R_t$. This metric represents the average quantity of secondary infections generated by an individual infected at a specific time $t$.

### Input Observations

The dataset provided covers an observation span of 150 days, commencing on 2023-01-01 and concluding on 2023-05-30. These observations are located in the file `data/cases.csv`. This file contains two columns: `date`, formatted as YYYY-MM-DD, and `cases`, which records integer counts.

### Fixed Epidemiological Parameters

Pre-established through external pathogen research, the following epidemiological distributions should be regarded as constant and certain, precluding any requirement for uncertainty propagation:

*   The generation interval adheres to a Gamma distribution, characterized by a mean of 5.5 days and a standard deviation of 2 days.
*   The reporting delay, which describes the duration from infection onset to case observation, follows a log-normal distribution with a mean of 5 days and a standard deviation of 2 days.

### Inherent Data Characteristics

Several structural attributes are present within the data:

*   A reporting delay exists between the point of infection and the observation of a case, as detailed by its distribution mentioned previously.
*   Case counts exhibit a cyclical weekly pattern, attributable to diminished reporting activities during weekends.
*   The ascertainment rate—the fraction of total infections captured as observed cases—is not constant over time. This temporal variation is unknown and necessitates estimation directly from the provided data.
*   The noise associated with observations displays overdispersion when compared against a standard homogeneous Poisson process.

### Implementation Requirements

Your primary deliverable is a set of daily point estimates for $R_t$, spanning the entirety of the observation period.

The foundation of your model *must* be the renewal equation. Your approach should establish a link between the unobserved infection process and the recorded case observations, incorporating the specified reporting delay. Explicit consideration must be given to:

*   The recurring day-of-week reporting patterns.
*   Fluctuations in ascertainment over time.
*   The overdispersed nature of observation noise, for instance, by employing a negative binomial distribution.

The choice of programming language or framework is unrestricted.

The conclusive $R_t$ estimates are to be stored in a file named `outputs/rt_estimates.csv`. This file must include the following columns:

*   `date` (in YYYY-MM-DD format)
*   `Rt_median` (representing the point estimate)
*   `Rt_lower` (an optional, but recommended, 5th-percentile credible or confidence bound)
*   `Rt_upper` (an optional, but recommended, 95th-percentile credible or confidence bound)

Should your code encounter errors, these are to be identified and resolved. In the event that the generated results appear inconsistent with expectations, the model should be refined and executed again.
