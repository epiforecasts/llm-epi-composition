# Dynamic Reproduction Number Estimation from Structured Observations

For an ongoing infectious disease outbreak, your objective is to determine the time-varying effective reproduction number, denoted as $R_t$. This metric quantifies the average number of secondary infections generated by an individual infected at time $t$, and it must be derived from the provided observational data.

## Input Data Specifications

Case observations are available within the file `data/cases.csv`. This dataset includes two columns: `date`, formatted as YYYY-MM-DD, and `cases`, representing integer counts. The total observation window spans 150 days, commencing on 2023-01-01 and concluding on 2023-05-30.

## Pathogen-Specific Parameters

The following epidemiological distributions are provided from external research on the pathogen. These should be considered as fixed, known values, with no requirement to incorporate their inherent uncertainty into your estimations.

*   **Generation Interval:** Characterized by a Gamma distribution with a mean of 5.5 days and a standard deviation of 2 days.
*   **Reporting Delay:** The time from infection onset to case observation follows a log-normal distribution, specified with a mean of 5 days and a standard deviation of 2 days.

## Inherent Data Characteristics

The observational data exhibits several structural properties that must be accounted for:

*   A delay exists between the actual infection event and its observation as a reported case, with its distribution detailed previously.
*   Case counts demonstrate a cyclical pattern corresponding to the day of the week, specifically due to diminished reporting during weekends.
*   The ascertainment rate, defined as the fraction of total infections that are successfully observed as cases, is not constant over time. This temporal variation is unknown and necessitates estimation directly from the supplied data.
*   The noise present in the observations displays overdispersion when compared to what would be expected from a simple homogeneous Poisson process.

## Assignment Requirements

Your primary output should be a set of daily point estimates for $R_t$, covering the entirety of the observation timeline.

The foundation of your model must be the renewal equation. It is essential to link the unobserved infection process to the reported case observations through the specified reporting delay. Your model must explicitly incorporate:

*   The cyclical reporting pattern tied to the day of the week.
*   The temporal variability in ascertainment.
*   Observation noise that exhibits overdispersion (for instance, by employing a negative binomial distribution).

All modeling should be implemented using the EpiAware.jl Julia package. Construct your model by utilizing the fundamental components and functionalities provided by EpiAware. Refer to the `epiaware_docs.md` file for details on available types and functions within the EpiAware API.

The computed $R_t$ estimates are to be saved in the file `outputs/rt_estimates.csv`. This output file requires the following columns:

*   `date`: Representing the date in YYYY-MM-DD format.
*   `Rt_median`: The central point estimate for $R_t$.
*   `Rt_lower`: (Optional, but recommended) The 5th-percentile credible or confidence bound.
*   `Rt_upper`: (Optional, but recommended) The 95th-percentile credible or confidence bound.

Should any errors occur during execution, they must be identified and resolved. Furthermore, if the resulting estimates appear to lack realism, the model should be refined and re-executed as necessary.
