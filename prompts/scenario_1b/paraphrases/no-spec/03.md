# Dynamic Reproduction Number Estimation

Your objective is to determine the time-varying effective reproduction number, denoted as $R_t$, for an infectious disease outbreak. This metric represents the average number of secondary infections generated by an individual infected at a specific time $t$. The estimation should be derived from the provided daily case counts.

## Input Data

The dataset spans a 150-day observation window, commencing on 2023-01-01 and concluding on 2023-05-30.
Case observations are supplied in the file `data/cases.csv`, which contains two columns:
- `date`: Formatted as YYYY-MM-DD.
- `cases`: Representing integer counts of daily cases.

## Pathogen Characteristics

The following epidemiological parameters, derived from external pathogen studies, should be treated as fixed and deterministic inputs; uncertainty propagation is not required for these values:
- The generation interval is characterized by a Gamma distribution with a mean of 5.5 days and a standard deviation of 2 days.
- The reporting delay, defined as the duration from infection to observed case, follows a log-normal distribution with a mean of 5 days and a standard deviation of 2 days.

## Data Characteristics

Several structural attributes of the observed data should be considered:
- A delay exists between the actual infection event and its observation as a reported case, with the distribution specified previously.
- Case counts are discrete integer values.
- Day-of-week patterns are present, indicating lower reporting volumes on weekends.
- The level of observation noise exceeds what a simple homogeneous Poisson model would predict.

## Estimation Procedure & Output

Your primary objective is to generate daily point estimates for $R_t$ covering the entire observation duration.

The methodological foundation for your model must be the renewal equation. This involves employing the interrelationship between $R_t$, the generation interval, and historical infection data to simulate the infection dynamics. Subsequently, the unobserved infection process must be linked to the observed case data through the reporting delay.

You are free to implement this solution using any programming language or framework.

The computed $R_t$ estimates are to be stored in the file `outputs/rt_estimates.csv`. This output file must include the following columns:
- `date`: In YYYY-MM-DD format.
- `Rt_median`: The central point estimate for $R_t$.
- `Rt_lower`: The 5th-percentile credible or confidence bound (inclusion is optional but recommended).
- `Rt_upper`: The 95th-percentile credible or confidence bound (inclusion is optional but recommended).

Should your implementation encounter runtime errors, you are expected to debug and resolve them. Furthermore, if the generated results appear to lack plausibility, you should refine your model and re-execute the estimation process.
