# Time-varying reproduction number estimation

You will analyze daily infectious disease outbreak counts to estimate the time-varying effective reproduction number $R_t$, defined as the mean number of secondary infections generated by a person infected at time $t$.

## Input data

Daily observations span 150 days from 2023-01-01 through 2023-05-30. Data location:

- `data/cases.csv` containing columns `date` (format: YYYY-MM-DD) and `cases` (integer values).

## Fixed epidemiological parameters

The following distributional parameters are derived from external pathogen studies and should be treated as known constants without uncertainty propagation:

- Generation interval: Gamma distribution with mean 5.5 days and standard deviation 2 days.
- Reporting delay (time from infection to case observation): log-normal distribution with mean 5 days and standard deviation 2 days.

## Data characteristics

- A reporting delay exists from infection to case observation, following the distribution specified above.
- Case counts are integers and display day-of-week effects reflecting reduced weekend reporting.
- Observation noise exceeds what a homogeneous Poisson model would predict.

## Requirements

Generate daily $R_t$ point estimates covering the entire observation period.

**Your model must be based on the renewal equation.** Specifically, use the relationship linking $R_t$, the generation interval, and historical infections to model the infection process, then link the latent infection process to observed cases through the reporting delay.

**Implementation language: Julia.** You may use standard Julia packages. Refer to `turing_api_docs.md` for Turing.jl API documentation; employ Turing.jl for probabilistic or Bayesian modeling tasks.

Write your final $R_t$ estimates to `outputs/rt_estimates.csv` with these columns:

- `date` (format: YYYY-MM-DD)
- `Rt_median` (point estimate)
- `Rt_lower` (5th-percentile credible/confidence bound, optional but recommended)
- `Rt_upper` (95th-percentile credible/confidence bound, optional but recommended)

Diagnose and resolve any errors your code produces. If results appear implausible, modify the model and execute again.
