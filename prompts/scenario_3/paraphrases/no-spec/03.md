# Unified Epidemic Reproduction Number Estimation

The objective of this task is to determine a single, time-varying effective reproduction number ($R_t$) for an infectious disease outbreak. This $R_t$ must be consistent across three distinct, independent daily observation streams: recorded cases, hospitalisations, and deaths, all reflecting the same underlying epidemic.

## Source Data

Daily counts are provided over a 150-day period, commencing on 2023-01-01 and concluding on 2023-05-30. The data files and their respective column structures are:

*   `data/cases.csv`: Contains `date` and `cases` columns.
*   `data/hospitalisations.csv`: Contains `date` and `hospitalisations` columns.
*   `data/deaths.csv`: Contains `date` and `deaths` columns.

## Epidemiological Parameters

The following distributions, derived from external pathogen studies, are to be treated as fixed and known values; no uncertainty propagation is required for these parameters:

*   **Generation Interval**: A Gamma distribution with a mean of 5.5 days and a standard deviation of 2 days.
*   **Reporting Delay (from infection to observation)**: Characterized by log-normal distributions:
    *   **Cases**: Mean of 5 days, standard deviation of 2 days.
    *   **Hospitalisations**: Mean of 10 days, standard deviation of 3 days.
    *   **Deaths**: Mean of 20 days, standard deviation of 5 days.

## Data and Model Characteristics

*   A single $R_t$ trajectory is shared across all three data series, which are independent observations of the same epidemic.
*   Each observation stream possesses its own distinct reporting delay (as specified above) and its own time-varying ascertainment rate, representing the proportion of infections eventually reported in that stream. These ascertainment trajectories are unknown.
*   The observed counts exhibit a recurring weekly reporting pattern.
*   Observation noise demonstrates overdispersion relative to a homogeneous Poisson process.

## Implementation Details

Your solution must produce daily point estimates for the unified $R_t$ trajectory. The infection process should be modeled using the renewal equation. Your approach must explicitly account for:

*   Stream-specific delays.
*   Time-varying, stream-specific ascertainment.
*   The day-of-week cycle.
*   Observation overdispersion.

Any programming language or framework may be utilized.

## Required Output

The final $R_t$ estimates must be saved to `outputs/rt_estimates.csv`. This file must contain the following columns:

*   `date`: In YYYY-MM-DD format.
*   `Rt_median`: The point estimate for $R_t$.
*   `Rt_lower`: The 5th-percentile credible or confidence bound (optional, but recommended).
*   `Rt_upper`: The 95th-percentile credible or confidence bound (optional, but recommended).

## Quality Assurance

Should your code encounter errors, you are responsible for diagnosing and correcting them. If the generated results appear implausible, adjustments to the model and subsequent re-execution are expected.
