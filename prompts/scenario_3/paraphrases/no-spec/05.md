# Joint estimation of time-varying reproduction number from three surveillance sources

You will work with daily infectious disease outbreak data comprising three observation types: cases, hospitalisations, and deaths. These three streams independently observe the same epidemic process. Your objective is to infer one time-varying effective reproduction number $R_t$ that reconciles all three observation types.

## Input data

Daily observations span 150 days from 2023-01-01 through 2023-05-30. Data files:

- `data/cases.csv` — containing columns `date`, `cases`.
- `data/hospitalisations.csv` — containing columns `date`, `hospitalisations`.
- `data/deaths.csv` — containing columns `date`, `deaths`.

## Fixed epidemiological parameters

The following parameters derive from external pathogen studies and should be treated as known constants without uncertainty propagation.

- Generation interval: Gamma-distributed with mean 5.5 days and standard deviation 2 days.
- Reporting delay (infection to observation), log-normal distribution:
  - Cases: mean 5 days, standard deviation 2 days.
  - Hospitalisations: mean 10 days, standard deviation 3 days.
  - Deaths: mean 20 days, standard deviation 5 days.

## Data structure

- The three observation streams independently reflect the same underlying epidemic and share one $R_t$ trajectory.
- Each stream possesses its own reporting delay (specified above) and its own time-varying ascertainment rate (the fraction of infections ultimately captured in that stream). Ascertainment varies over time, differs between streams, and is not known.
- Weekly reporting cycles are present in the counts.
- Observation noise exhibits overdispersion compared to a homogeneous Poisson process.

## Requirements

Generate daily point estimates for one $R_t$ trajectory that is consistent across all three observation streams. Apply the renewal equation to model the infection process. Explicitly incorporate stream-specific delays, stream-specific time-varying ascertainment, day-of-week effects, and overdispersed observations.

Any language or framework is permitted.

Write final $R_t$ estimates to `outputs/rt_estimates.csv` with these columns:

- `date` (YYYY-MM-DD format)
- `Rt_median` (point estimate)
- `Rt_lower` (5th-percentile credible/confidence bound, optional but encouraged)
- `Rt_upper` (95th-percentile credible/confidence bound, optional but encouraged)

Diagnose and resolve any errors your code encounters. If results appear implausible, modify the model and execute again.
