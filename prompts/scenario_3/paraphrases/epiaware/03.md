# Estimation of a Shared Time-Varying Reproduction Number from Diverse Observation Streams

Your objective is to determine a single, dynamic effective reproduction number ($R_t$) for an infectious disease outbreak, utilizing daily reported figures from three distinct yet related measurement series: cases, hospitalisations, and deaths. All three streams represent independent observations stemming from the same underlying epidemic process and must be integrated to derive a consistent $R_t$ trajectory.

## Input Data Streams

Data collection spans 150 days, from 2023-01-01 to 2023-05-30. The necessary data files are located at:

- `data/cases.csv` — Contains `date` and `cases` columns.
- `data/hospitalisations.csv` — Contains `date` and `hospitalisations` columns.
- `data/deaths.csv` — Contains `date` and `deaths` columns.

## Fixed Epidemiological Parameters

These distributional properties for the pathogen are sourced from external research and should be treated as fixed values; uncertainty propagation for these parameters is not required.

-   **Generation Interval**: Follows a Gamma distribution with a mean of 5.5 days and a standard deviation of 2 days.
-   **Reporting Delay (Infection to Observation)**: Modeled as a log-normal distribution, with stream-specific parameters:
    -   **Cases**: Mean of 5 days, standard deviation of 2 days.
    -   **Hospitalisations**: Mean of 10 days, standard deviation of 3 days.
    -   **Deaths**: Mean of 20 days, standard deviation of 5 days.

## Inherent Data Characteristics

The following structural features and assumptions characterize the data:

-   All three distinct measurement series are generated by the same underlying epidemic and converge on a singular $R_t$ progression.
-   Each observation stream possesses its own unique reporting delay (as specified above) and exhibits dynamic ascertainment rates, which represent the proportion of infections eventually captured by that specific stream. These ascertainment patterns vary across streams and are not provided.
-   The daily counts in each stream exhibit a periodic weekly pattern.
-   Observation noise in the counts features overdispersion relative to a homogeneous Poisson process.

## Core Task Description

Generate daily point estimates for a singular $R_t$ trajectory that accurately reflects all three data sources. The infection process should be modeled via the renewal equation. Explicitly incorporate stream-specific delays, dynamic ascertainment rates specific to each stream, the weekly periodicity, and overdispersed observation noise into your model.

The implementation must leverage the `EpiAware.jl` Julia package. Construct your model using the primitives available within EpiAware. Refer to `epiaware_docs.md` for the EpiAware API documentation, detailing available types and functions.

Your final $R_t$ estimates must be saved to the file path `outputs/rt_estimates.csv`, structured with the following columns:

-   `date` (in YYYY-MM-DD format)
-   `Rt_median` (the central point estimate)
-   `Rt_lower` (the 5th-percentile credible/confidence bound; inclusion is optional but recommended)
-   `Rt_upper` (the 95th-percentile credible/confidence bound; inclusion is optional but recommended)

Should errors arise during execution, diagnose and rectify them. In cases where the estimated results appear implausible, refine the model and re-execute the process.
